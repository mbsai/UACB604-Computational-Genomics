Genomics maths

From strings to a genotype matrix

We rarely compare whole strings. We compare each person to the reference genome and keep only the differences (variants).

positionrefaltAshaBenChenDev
chr1:1,014,143CT0102
chr1:1,020,172GA1120
chr2:3,901,551AG0001

Toy data. 0 / 1 / 2 = number of copies of the alternative allele (humans carry two copies of each chromosome).

The result is a matrix \(X \in \{0,1,2\}^{n \times p}\). The 1000 Genomes Project: \(n \approx 2{,}500\) people, \(p \approx 88\) million variants.

Hardy–Weinberg: with allele frequency \(q\), genotypes 0, 1, 2 should appear with probabilities \((1-q)^2,\ 2q(1-q),\ q^2\). Deviations flag genotyping errors or selection.
4 min. Allele frequency of SNP 1: (0+1+0+2)/(2×4) = 3/8. Preview: PCA on X separates populations (Unit 10/multi-omics).
Transcriptomics interactive maths

A count matrix, and why raw counts lie

RNA-seq counts how many reads land on each gene: a matrix of non-negative integers, genes × samples.

Samples A and B come from identical cells. Only the sequencing depth differs. Drag the slider.

Depth of sample B: 1.0× sample A
Counts per million (CPM): divide by the library size \(N_j\): $$\text{CPM}_{ij} = \frac{K_{ij}}{N_j}\times 10^6$$
But if one gene in B is hugely up-regulated, it “steals” reads and every other gene looks down. Better normalisations (DESeq2's median of ratios, TMM) fix this. Unit 10.

Longer genes also collect more reads, so comparing genes within a sample needs length normalisation (TPM).

4 min. Ask before moving the slider: would a naive t-test call gene X differentially expressed? Raw counts scale with depth; CPM is constant.
Transcriptomics interactive maths

Counts are noisier than Poisson

If reads were sampled independently, counts would be Poisson: \(\operatorname{Var} = \mu\).

Biological replicates vary more. The negative binomial adds a dispersion \(\phi\): $$\operatorname{Var} = \mu + \phi\,\mu^{2}$$

dispersion \(\phi\) = 0.10

Dots: sample mean vs variance of each simulated gene (log–log). Grey line: Poisson. Red line: negative binomial.

4 min. Set φ = 0: dots sit on the Poisson line. Raise φ: high-count genes peel away. Real RNA-seq data look like φ ≈ 0.05–0.2 — students will see this in the notebook.

Epigenomics interactive maths

Methylation is a proportion, with error bars

Bisulfite sequencing turns unmethylated C into T. At each CpG site we count reads: \(k\) still read C (methylated) out of \(n\).

Methylation level \(\hat\beta = k/n\). That is a binomial proportion, and its uncertainty depends on coverage \(n\).

The human genome has about 28 million CpG sites, so the data are a long vector of proportions along the genome: a signal track. ChIP-seq (where a protein binds) gives a similar track of read coverage.

coverage n = 20 reads methylated reads k = 14

5 min. Set n = 4, k = 3: β̂ = 0.75 but the interval is huge. Ask: how much coverage do we need for ±0.1? Roughly n ≈ 100. This is a design question — Unit 5 on coverage.
Metagenomics interactive maths

A mixture, measured as proportions

A gut sample holds DNA from hundreds of species. Sequencing gives relative abundances: each sample sums to 1. That is compositional data.

Grow only species A by 1×
Species B, C and D did not change at all, yet their proportions fell. Correlations between proportions are therefore misleading. Fix: log-ratio methods (Aitchison). Unit 12.
5 min. Ask: if a paper says “species B decreased in patients”, what else could explain it? (Something else increased.)
Why it matters biology

Omics in human health

Cancer

  • Genomics: compare tumour vs normal DNA of the same patient to find somatic mutations.
  • Transcriptomics: PAM50 uses the expression of 50 genes to sort breast cancers into intrinsic subtypes that respond differently to treatment.
  • Epigenomics: tumour-specific methylation in blood DNA is the basis of liquid biopsy tests.

Type 2 diabetes

  • Genomics: genome-wide association studies have found hundreds of risk loci, each with a small effect.
  • Metagenomics: gut microbiomes differ in T2D, but much of the early signal came from the drug metformin, not the disease (Forslund et al., Nature 2015).
Lesson for statisticians: always ask about confounders.
