Why read length matters biology maths

Short reads get lost in repeats

A read is placed uniquely only if it contains unique sequence (Lecture 1.1: k-mers become unique at \(k \gtrsim \log_4 n\)). A read lying entirely inside a repeat can't be placed.

Human repeats: Alu ~300 bp (a million copies), full-length LINE-1 ~6 kb, centromeric arrays spanning megabases.

3 min. This is the motivation for the whole lecture, and for long-read assembly in Units 6–7.
PacBio SMRT sequencing interactive maths

HiFi: read one molecule many times

One polymerase sits at the bottom of a tiny well (a zero-mode waveguide) and copies one DNA molecule; each incorporated nucleotide flashes its colour. Millions of wells are watched at once.

The DNA is made circular (a “SMRTbell”), so the polymerase goes round and round: one insert read in many passes. Single passes are noisy (~10% errors, mostly insertions/deletions); a consensus of many passes is very accurate.

Majority vote is wrong only if more than half the passes are wrong:

$$\sum_{k>n/2}\binom{n}{k}e^k(1-e)^{n-k}$$

Simplified model: real errors are mostly indels and the consensus uses a statistical model, but the trend is the same. HiFi reads: typically 15–20 kb at Q30 or better.

6 min. The sum is the binomial distribution (primer §8): k wrong passes out of n. With e = 0.1: 1 pass Q10, 5 passes Q21, 9 passes Q31.
Oxford Nanopore interactive maths

Reading DNA as an electric current

sequence:

A motor protein ratchets single-stranded DNA through a protein pore in a membrane. Ions flowing through the pore give a current that depends on the ~5–9 bases inside it at that moment (a k-mer).

The raw current trace (the “squiggle”) is turned into bases by a neural network (basecallers such as Guppy and its successor Dorado).

Try the homopolymer: eight A's give one long flat step, the same as four A's but longer. Counting bases means estimating time, and that's noisy. Homopolymer lengths are a classic nanopore error.

6 min. This simulator gives each 5-mer a made-up current level; real tables are learned from data. Reads can be very long: >100 kb is routine, records exceed a megabase. Portable (MinION, USB-powered).

Linked reads interactive maths

Long-range information from short reads

Long DNA molecules (tens of kb) are each tagged with a barcode; all short reads from one molecule carry the same barcode. Sequenced on an ordinary Illumina machine, they form “virtual long reads”.

  • 10x Genomics Chromium linked reads: barcodes in tiny droplets (discontinued in 2020)
  • TELL-Seq: barcoded beads in a single tube, no special instrument
  • Uses: phasing haplotypes, scaffolding assemblies (e.g. the Supernova assembler, Unit 6)

Catch: two molecules may get the same barcode, like two people sharing a birthday.

molecules \(m\) = barcodes \(N\) =
P(at least one shared barcode)
expected shared pairs
$$P \approx 1 - e^{-m(m-1)/2N}, \quad E = \frac{m(m-1)}{2N}$$

Try m = 23, N = 365: the classic birthday puzzle (≈ 50%).

5 min. Collisions grow with m², not m. That's why protocols load a small amount of DNA per barcode set.

Today in one slide

  • Long reads span repeats that defeat short reads.
  • PacBio HiFi: many noisy passes → accurate consensus (binomial majority vote).
  • Nanopore: current from a k-mer in a pore → neural-network basecalling; homopolymers are hard.
