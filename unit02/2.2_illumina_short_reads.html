How much sequencing is one human genome?

Clinical whole-genome sequencing aims for 30× coverage: every base read about 30 times on average.

The key formula (more in Unit 5)

$$\text{coverage } C = \frac{N \times L}{G}$$

\(N\) reads of length \(L\) on a genome of size \(G\).

Sanger: ~1,000 bases per reaction. To reach 93 billion bases you need massive parallelism: billions of reactions side by side on a glass slide.

3 min. 3.1 × 10⁹ × 30 = 9.3 × 10¹⁰ = 93 Gb. With 150 bp reads that is 620 million reads.
How it works biology

Sequencing by synthesis in four steps

1 · Library

Cut DNA into fragments (a few hundred bp); attach adapters to both ends.

2 · Clusters

Fragments bind to a flow cell and are copied in place (bridge amplification) into clusters of ~1,000 identical copies.

3 · One base per cycle

Add fluorescent nucleotides with a removable terminator: each strand grows by exactly one base, then pauses.

4 · Image, cleave, repeat

A camera records each cluster's colour; dye and terminator are removed; repeat for 50–300 cycles.

Hundreds of millions to billions of clusters are read in parallel. Newer instruments use two dyes instead of four (e.g. a base is read as “both”, “red only”, “green only” or “dark”).

5 min. Your home ground. Key idea for the maths: every cluster is ~1,000 copies that must stay in step.

Why quality drops interactive maths

Phasing: losing step, cycle by cycle

chance a copy falls behind each cycle: \(\varepsilon\) =

Each copy in a cluster stays in step with probability \(1-\varepsilon\) per cycle (AND rule), so after \(n\) cycles:

$$\text{in step} = (1-\varepsilon)^n$$

Half the cluster is out of step when \((1-\varepsilon)^n = \tfrac12\):

$$n_{1/2} = \frac{\ln \tfrac12}{\ln(1-\varepsilon)} \approx \frac{0.693}{\varepsilon}$$
5 min. Same maths as radioactive half-life. This is one reason Illumina reads are 50–300 bases, not thousands.

Paired-end reads

fragment length
read length

Read 1 from one end, read 2 from the other (pointing inward). We know roughly how far apart they are.

Overlap \(= 2L - F\) when positive; otherwise an unread gap of \(F - 2L\).

Why pairs help: if read 1 falls in a repeat, read 2 often lands in unique sequence and anchors it; unusual distances between mates reveal deletions and rearrangements.

4 min. Try F = 250, L = 150: overlap 50 bp, so the pair can be merged into one 250 bp read.

Illumina in numbers (typical)

  • Reads 50–300 bases; often 2 × 150
  • Errors mostly substitutions, ~0.1–1% per base, rising along the read
  • From millions of reads (MiSeq) to billions (NovaSeq-class) per run
  • Cheapest per base of all methods: the workhorse for RNA-seq, exomes, resequencing
